How much do heat and humidity slow your running pace?

Updated 2026-10-04 · Figures from the ArcPace weather model

Short answer: add the air temperature and the dew point in °F. Below about 120 the weather costs nothing, and a cool, dry day even helps; at 120 it's a few seconds. Above that, the cost climbs fast: at a 10:00/mile easy pace, a sum of 140 adds about 24 seconds per mile, 150 adds about 40, and 160 about 55. Slower runners lose more: on an 80°F morning with a 65°F dew point, about 19 s/mi at 8:00 pace, 32 s/mi at 10:00 and 49 s/mi at 12:00.

Sweat cools you only when it evaporates, and humid air slows that. Dew point measures the water in the air directly, so it predicts a run better than temperature or relative humidity.

Why slower runners lose more #

Temperature-plus-dew-point charts come from competitive race results. But in marathon data (Ely and colleagues, 2007), leaders lost under 1% per 5°C of WBGT while runners further back lost over 3%: slower runners spend longer in the heat and make less cooling airflow. ArcPace scales the heat cost to your easy pace, from 0.8× for fast runners to 2.2× at about 13:40/mile.

How these numbers are made #

  • Pace cost is a percentage of your easy pace, from a temperature + dew point table, measured against an ordinary day (a sum of 120, about 60°F with a 60°F dew point, or a little warmer with drier air).
  • In sun, a radiant term is added from solar radiation, the same one used for WBGT.
  • When there's heat to shed, still air costs a little extra: a runner relies on airflow to cool.
  • Default heat tolerance (3 of 5). ArcPace lets you set yours from 1 to 5; each step moves the temperature + dew point sum by 6°F.
  • Wind, rain, snow and altitude add their own costs in the app; these tables hold them at 5 mph, dry and sea level.

These are estimates for planning easy runs, not medical advice. In real heat, slow down by feel, drink, and stop if you feel unwell. For race-day risk, see WBGT and heat flags; for when to stop, heat illness warning signs; for how to stay cool, how to run in the heat.

Questions #

How much slower should I run in heat and humidity?

Add the temperature and dew point in °F. Below about 120 there's no cost, and at 120 only a few seconds. At a 10:00/mile easy pace, 140 adds about 24 seconds per mile, 150 about 40 and 160 about 55. Slower paces lose more.

Is dew point or humidity better for running?

Dew point. Relative humidity depends on the temperature, so 90% at dawn can be comfortable and 50% in the afternoon miserable. Dew point measures the water in the air directly: under about 55°F feels fine, 60–65°F is noticeable, and 70°F or more is oppressive.

How much does 80 degrees slow running?

It depends on the dew point and your pace. At 80°F with a 65°F dew point, in shade, about 19 s/mi at 8:00 pace, 32 s/mi at 10:00 and 49 s/mi at 12:00.

Does running in the sun make a difference?

Yes. Full sun adds a radiant load. At a 10:00/mile pace, 75°F with a 70°F dew point costs about 32 s/mi in shade and 36 s/mi in sun.

Do you run faster in cool weather?

A little. Against an ordinary 60°F day, a cool, dry morning (50°F, dew point 35°F) is worth about 7 seconds per mile faster at a 10:00 easy pace.
